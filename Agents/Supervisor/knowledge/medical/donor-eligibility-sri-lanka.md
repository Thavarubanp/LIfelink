# Who can donate blood

A blood donor should be a healthy volunteer. In Sri Lanka the National Blood Transfusion Service (NBTS) publishes the general criteria below. The blood bank doctor always makes the final decision after the health questionnaire and a short physical check.

- **Age:** 18 to 60 years.
- **Body weight:** at least 50 kg.
- **Haemoglobin:** must meet the blood bank's minimum level (checked with a finger-prick test on the day; commonly around 12.5 g/dL).
- **General health:** feeling well on the day, with no fever, cold, cough or other infection.
- **Donation interval:** at least 4 months (120 days) between whole blood donations, so the body can rebuild iron stores.

# Before you donate

- Sleep at least 6 hours the night before.
- Eat a normal meal within the 4 hours before donating; do not donate on an empty stomach.
- Drink plenty of water.
- Avoid alcohol for 24 hours before donating.
- Bring your NIC or passport.

# Reasons you may be asked to wait (temporary deferral)

- Pregnancy, recent delivery or breastfeeding.
- A current infection, fever, or a course of antibiotics that has not finished.
- Recent surgery, a recent blood transfusion, a tattoo, body piercing or acupuncture (commonly a wait of up to 12 months).
- Recent travel to a malaria-endemic area or a recent malaria or dengue illness.
- Some vaccinations and medicines.

A temporary deferral protects both the donor and the patient. It does not mean you can never donate.

# Reasons you may not be able to donate

Some conditions and risks lead to permanent deferral, for example a history of HIV, hepatitis B or hepatitis C infection, injecting recreational drugs, and some serious heart, lung, kidney, liver or blood disorders. The doctor explains the decision in private.

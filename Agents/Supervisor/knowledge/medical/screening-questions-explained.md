# Why these questions are asked

Every screening question protects either you (the donor) or the patient who will receive your blood. Answer honestly; your answers are only shared with the doctor who reviews your donation. The doctor, not the AI assistant, decides whether you can donate.

# Terms in the medical history section

- **G6PD deficiency:** an inherited enzyme deficiency that makes red cells break down more easily, especially with some medicines or foods. It can affect the patient who receives the blood.
- **Polycythaemia:** a condition in which the body makes too many red cells. Blood from some forms is not used for transfusion.
- **Blood disorders:** conditions such as thalassaemia, sickle cell disease or leukaemia.
- **Bleeding disorders:** conditions such as haemophilia or von Willebrand disease, where blood does not clot normally. Donating could be risky for you.
- **Epilepsy or seizures:** a history of fits; recent seizures or medicines for them may mean you should not donate.
- **Tuberculosis (TB):** a bacterial infection, usually of the lungs. Donation waits until treatment is complete and you are well.
- **Leprosy, syphilis and gonorrhoea:** infections that can affect whether blood can be used.
- **Hepatitis B and C, jaundice:** liver infections (jaundice is yellowing of the skin or eyes) that can pass through blood.

# Terms in the recent events and medication sections

- **Acupuncture:** treatment with fine needles; needles that may not be sterile can carry infection.
- **Rabies treatment:** injections given after a possible rabies exposure, such as a dog bite.
- **Blood thinners (anticoagulants):** medicines such as warfarin that stop clotting.
- **Anti-platelet medicines:** such as aspirin or clopidogrel; they matter mostly for platelet donation.
- **Steroids:** medicines such as prednisolone that reduce inflammation.
- **Anti-malarial medication:** medicines taken to prevent or treat malaria, usually linked to travel.
- **Malaria-endemic country:** a country where malaria is common.

# The confidential section

Questions about HIV, hepatitis, sexually transmitted infections, injected drugs and high-risk behaviour are asked of every donor, in confidence. Some infections do not show up in tests straight after exposure, so these answers protect patients. A "yes" does not mean you are judged; the doctor will discuss it privately.

# Stock levels

A hospital blood bank keeps enough of each blood group to cover its normal usage plus emergencies, without holding so much that units expire.

- **Minimum level (threshold):** the stock below which the hospital should reorder or ask for a transfer. A common approach is to set it to a few days of typical use for that group.
- **Maximum level:** the most the hospital can store and use before expiry.
- Groups used most often (for example O+) need higher thresholds; O- is also kept as an emergency reserve.

# Preventing wastage

- Issue the earliest-expiring units first (first-expired, first-out).
- Review units that will expire soon every day.
- Offer units that will not be used in time to hospitals or patients who need that group, before they expire.
- Track expired units and the reasons, and adjust ordering and thresholds.

# Sharing between hospitals

Regional sharing moves units from hospitals with surplus to hospitals with shortages. Transfers keep the cold chain, keep each unit's identity and records, and should be agreed by both blood banks.

# Monitoring

Monitor stock against thresholds for every group, units expiring soon, and demand from open requests. Early alerts give time to arrange transfers or donor calls before a shortage becomes critical.

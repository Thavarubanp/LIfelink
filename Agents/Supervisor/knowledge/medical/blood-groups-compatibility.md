# Blood groups

Blood groups are defined by the ABO system (A, B, AB, O) and the RhD factor (positive or negative), giving eight common groups: A+, A-, B+, B-, AB+, AB-, O+ and O-.

# Red cell compatibility (who can receive from whom)

| Patient | Can receive red cells from |
|---|---|
| O- | O- |
| O+ | O+, O- |
| A- | A-, O- |
| A+ | A+, A-, O+, O- |
| B- | B-, O- |
| B+ | B+, B-, O+, O- |
| AB- | AB-, A-, B-, O- |
| AB+ | all groups |

- **O negative** red cells can be given to patients of any group, which is why O- is called the universal red cell donor and is kept for emergencies.
- **AB positive** patients can receive red cells from every group (universal recipient).
- A negative (Rh-) patient should receive Rh-negative red cells, especially women who may become pregnant.

# Plasma is the other way round

For plasma, AB is the universal donor and O is the universal recipient. LifeLink requests are for whole blood / red cells, so LifeLink uses the red cell table above.

# Knowing your blood group

If you do not know your blood group, the blood bank tests it when you donate. In LifeLink, the tested group recorded at your first donation becomes your confirmed blood group.

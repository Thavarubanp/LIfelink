# What happens on the day

1. Registration and identity check.
2. Health questionnaire (in LifeLink this is completed with the screening assistant beforehand) and a private check with the doctor.
3. A mini physical: temperature, pulse, blood pressure and haemoglobin.
4. The donation itself takes about 8 to 10 minutes; the whole visit usually takes under an hour. One unit of whole blood is collected (LifeLink records each unit as a 440 ml packet).
5. Rest and refreshments for 10 to 15 minutes.

# After donating

- Drink extra fluids for the next 24 to 48 hours.
- Avoid strenuous exercise and heavy lifting for the rest of the day.
- Keep the bandage on for a few hours; if the needle site bleeds, press on it and raise your arm.
- If you feel dizzy or light-headed, sit or lie down with your feet raised until it passes.
- Eat iron-rich foods to help replace iron.

# How the body recovers

The fluid part of the blood (plasma) is replaced within about a day. Red cells take several weeks to be fully replaced, which is why a minimum interval between whole blood donations is required (120 days in LifeLink).

# When to seek help

Contact the blood bank or a doctor if you feel unwell after donating, if the arm is very painful or swollen, or if you become ill in the days after donation (so the blood bank can decide whether the donated unit is safe to use).

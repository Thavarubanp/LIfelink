# Storage temperature

Whole blood and red cells are stored between **+2 °C and +6 °C** in dedicated blood bank refrigerators with temperature monitoring and alarms. Platelets are kept at 20 to 24 °C with gentle agitation, and plasma is frozen.

# Shelf life of whole blood and red cells

The shelf life depends on the anticoagulant-preservative bag:

- **CPD:** about 21 days.
- **CPDA-1:** about 35 days.
- **Additive solutions (for example SAGM) for red cells:** up to 42 days.

This is why LifeLink lets each hospital set a packet shelf life between 21 and 35 days, matching the bags it uses.

# Keeping the cold chain

- Record the temperature of storage and transport equipment regularly.
- Transport blood in validated cool boxes and keep the transport time short.
- Blood that has been outside controlled storage for too long (many blood banks use a 30-minute rule) must not be returned to stock without checks.
- Never use blood past its expiry date.

# Using stock before it expires

Issue the packets that expire first (first-expired, first-out). When a hospital has packets close to expiry that it will not use, sharing them with another hospital that needs that group prevents wastage.

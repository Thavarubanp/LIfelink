# Emergency release of blood

When a patient is bleeding heavily and there is no time to test their group, hospitals can release **group O red cells** at once. O negative is preferred for girls and women who may become pregnant; many hospitals use O positive for others to protect O negative stock. The patient is switched to their own group as soon as it is known.

# Major bleeding protocols

Hospitals use a major haemorrhage (massive transfusion) protocol so that red cells, plasma and platelets arrive quickly and in agreed ratios, with the blood bank told early.

# When stock is short

1. Use the hospital's own stock first, earliest-expiring units first.
2. Ask nearby hospitals that hold compatible blood for an urgent transfer.
3. Call suitable donors: compatible blood group, meeting the donation interval and fit to donate.
4. Keep the patient's doctor informed about how many units are available and when more will arrive.

# Donors in an emergency

Emergency donors go through the same health screening as any other donor. An emergency never relaxes the safety checks that protect the donor and the patient.

# Purpose of donor selection

Donor selection has two goals: to protect the health of the donor, and to protect the patient who receives the blood. WHO recommends a confidential health questionnaire and interview before every donation, and relying on voluntary, unpaid donors.

# Temporary and permanent deferral

A **temporary deferral** means waiting for a set time (for example after an infection, a tattoo or travel) before donating again. A **permanent deferral** applies when donating would put the donor or the patient at lasting risk.

# Common temporary deferrals (typical periods; national rules apply)

- **Tattoo, body piercing or acupuncture:** commonly deferred for 6 to 12 months, because of the risk of blood-borne infection.
- **Receiving a blood transfusion:** commonly 12 months.
- **Surgery:** until fully recovered; after major surgery, commonly 6 to 12 months.
- **Pregnancy:** deferred during pregnancy and usually for at least 6 months after delivery; also while breastfeeding.
- **Antibiotics and infections:** until the course is finished and the infection has cleared.
- **Malaria risk:** people who travelled to or lived in malaria-endemic areas, or who had malaria, are deferred for a period that depends on the exposure; the blood bank applies its own rule.
- **Vaccinations:** live attenuated vaccines usually need a short wait (often about 4 weeks); rabies vaccine given after an exposure usually needs a longer wait (often 12 months).
- **Contact with someone who has hepatitis:** commonly 12 months after the last close contact.

# Permanent deferrals

WHO lists conditions that normally prevent donation permanently, including infection with HIV, hepatitis B or hepatitis C, injecting drug use, and certain serious chronic illnesses (for example some heart, lung, kidney or liver diseases, cancers and bleeding disorders). A history of syphilis also leads to deferral; services differ on whether this is permanent.

# Donor care

Donors should be at least 18 years old, meet the minimum weight and haemoglobin levels, and be informed about what happens during donation and how to look after themselves afterwards.
